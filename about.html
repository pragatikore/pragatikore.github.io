About

A little bit of context.

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in statistical genetics with a passion for building computational methods that make genomics more equitable. Here’s a little about what I’m working on, how I got here, and what I enjoy outside the lab.

Currently

I’m a Ph.D. candidate in the Genetics & Genomics program at Baylor College of Medicine, in the Bioinformatics, Genomics and Systems Biology track, working with Dr. Elizabeth Atkinson. My dissertation is about advancing genomic equity through local ancestry inference — figuring out, block by block along a chromosome, which ancestral population a given segment of DNA most likely came from, and using that to make resources like gnomAD more accurate for people with admixed ancestry.

I also completed a Clinical Translational Research Certificate of Added Qualifications (Sept 2025) alongside the Ph.D., which is where a side project on polygenic risk scores for atrial fibrillation in Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome came from.

Before Baylor

I worked as an Associate Researcher in the Sebra Lab, Department of Genetics and Genomic Sciences, at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, and earned my B.S. in Biochemistry from the University of Texas at Austin, where I also picked up a certificate in Elements of Computing.

Off the clock

I’m a coffee enthusiast (my latte art is definitely improving!), a foodie who loves exploring new cafés and restaurants, and I spend a lot of my free time trying new recipes, planning my next trip, or playing pickleball.
