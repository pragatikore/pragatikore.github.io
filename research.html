Research

Making reference genomes
look more like everyone.

Most population-genetic tools were built and tuned on a narrow slice of ancestries. I work on closing that gap — using local ancestry inference and rare-variant methods to make genomic resources like gnomAD more accurate for people with admixed backgrounds.

Dissertation

Advancing genomic equity through local ancestry inference, advised by Dr. Elizabeth Atkinson, Dept. of Molecular and Human Genetics, BCM (Jan 2023 – anticipated Dec 2026). Two complementary threads: a public resource that makes reference allele frequencies ancestry-aware, and a method that carries the same idea into rare-variant discovery.

Part 1 · with the Broad Institute of MIT & Harvard

Improved allele frequencies in gnomAD through local ancestry inference

Introduced local ancestry–informed allele frequencies into gnomAD for its two most admixed groups — Admixed American (~7,600 individuals) and African/African American (~20,000) — the first time local ancestry has been systematically built into a globally representative genomic resource. The payoff is sharper, ancestry-resolved frequency estimates: roughly 80% of variants show at least a two-fold difference between ancestral backgrounds, which directly changes how disease-risk variants are interpreted in people with admixed ancestry. Presented as invited talks at BCM, UCLA, the Broad Institute, and Cassyni Research Seminars.

Part 2 · Tractor-Burden

Improving rare variant discovery in admixed populations

Built Tractor-Burden, an ancestry-aware rare-variant burden testing framework for admixed populations. Most burden tests assume a single, homogeneous ancestry, which loses power in admixed genomes where variant frequencies and effects differ across ancestral tracts. By aggregating ancestry-specific rare-variant dosages within user-defined regions and running ancestry-specific burden tests, it improves detection of risk genes whose burden is concentrated in one ancestry; in simulations, modeling these ancestry-specific effects increases discovery power. Released as open-source Python script with a step-by-step tutorial.

CTR-CAQ Capstone · with Dr. Seema Lalani & Dr. Christina Miyake

Risk stratification in Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome

Built a polygenic risk score (PRS) for atrial fibrillation to risk-stratify patients with Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) syndrome — a conduction disorder that predisposes to life-threatening arrhythmias, though its genetic link to AF is still unknown. Constructed, optimized, and applied an AF PRS from GWAS summary statistics to whole-exome sequencing from a 305-patient WPW cohort, and found a significant association between AF genetic risk and WPW that separated patients from 1000 Genomes controls. The result points toward using genetic predisposition to guide monitoring, tailored treatment, and prevention — a step toward precision cardiology.

Research interests

Rare variants

Methods for finding and interpreting rare genetic variation, where sample size and ancestry composition matter most.

Local ancestry inference

Segmenting genomes by ancestral origin to improve frequency and association estimates in admixed individuals.

Health equity in genomics

Making sure the databases clinicians rely on actually reflect the diversity of the patients they’re used for.
